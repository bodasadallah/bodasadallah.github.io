<h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22975" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Large Language Models Have Not Yet Transformed Science</a></h3>
<div class="dn-why">Large-scale full-text analysis of pre/post-LLM lexical and structural shifts in scientific writing; relevant to human-vs-LLM scientific writing.</div>
<details><summary>Abstract</summary><p>The emergence of large language models (LLMs) in late 2022 has raised questions about the future of science, but assessing their impact requires understanding how scientific practices were already evolving. We analyze 2.5 million full-text papers across five disciplines from 2015 to 2025, tracing trends over a broad spectrum of lexical, structural, epistemological, and social dimensions. Aside from frequency shifts in some words, most pre-2023 trends continued with limited detectable breaks. Among the notable exceptions, growth in computer science coauthor teams accelerated significantly in Asia, while apparent declines in biomedical topic diversity largely reflected standardization in article formats. Our study establishes an empirical foundation for monitoring the health of global science as LLM adoption deepens.</p></details>
