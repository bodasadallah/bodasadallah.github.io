<div class="dn-lede">This digest covers about a day. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, and Fable 5.2 has taken back the top spot on Artificial Analysis. The item that matters most for your own work is a venue policy: TACL now accepts only one new submission every three months from any first author, and says this is because of a flood of LLM-generated papers.</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Big news</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://bsky.app/profile/bluenoser2.bsky.social/post/3mzv5uxqfuc2e" target="_blank" rel="noopener">This is a first for a MIT Press journal I think. Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics is limiting submissions from…</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">NLP feed on Bluesky · Nov 09 · 13 likes, 6 reposts</div>
<p>TACL will now take one new submission every three months per first author, with some exceptions; invited resubmissions do not count. A post sharing the news says it is meant to stem a flood of LLM-generated papers and may be a first for an MIT Press journal. It is a concrete venue response to AI-generated submissions overloading peer review, which you could cite or study.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hacker News · Nov 09 · 975 points, 6 comments</div>
<p>Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 (975 points on Hacker News), claiming new highs in coding, finance and the sciences. Anthropic also says the model 'solved two open problems in the physics of black holes', which is so far only a company claim. Latent Space reports a 20% cut in per-token price ($8/$40 per million tokens), but cost per task on SemiAnalysis's GDPx benchmark went up ($88 vs $67), because the model writes far more tests and spins up subagents to check its own work.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Models and releases</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/10/muse-ember/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Introducing Our Smallest, Most Efficient Model: Muse Ember</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Simon Willison · Nov 10</div>
<p>Meta launched Muse Ember, its smallest and most efficient model, aimed at tool calls, extraction and long-document parsing, with a context of up to a million tokens. For short prompts it costs $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, the same as GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5; the price rises above 200k tokens. That makes it a cheap option for running extraction over large corpora of papers or reviews.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Research and techniques</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://bsky.app/profile/emollick.bsky.social/post/3mzsyjxxrsk2l" target="_blank" rel="noopener">What happens to model performance on the popular DeepSWE coding benchmark if you make small alterations to the questions, like changing a…</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Ethan Mollick on Bluesky · Nov 08 · 142 likes, 16 reposts</div>
<p>Small edits to DeepSWE coding-benchmark questions, such as lowercasing a file name, pull apart models that had looked about equally good. It is a robustness warning for anyone ranking LLMs, LLM judges or AI reviewers on a fixed benchmark.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12762" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Sage-Taiyi: A Metacognitive Architecture for Scientific Discovery</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hugging Face daily papers · Nov 08 · 18 upvotes</div>
<p>Sage-Taiyi, a preprint, proposes an AI co-scientist built around metacognition: it keeps track of evidence, assumptions and open questions, and uses them to choose what to investigate next. It is evaluated on Taiyi-Bench, a new ideation benchmark of 265 tasks drawn from 50 papers across ten fields. The benchmark itself may interest you as a way of evaluating AI-generated scientific content.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/10/prompting-claude-fable-52/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Quoting Prompting Claude Fable 5.2</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Simon Willison · Nov 10</div>
<p>Anthropic's prompting guide for Fable 5.2 reports that telling the model the codebase is its own raised the rate at which it merged repeated code from under a tenth of tries to about half. It is a measured example of how one framing sentence can change model behaviour, which is worth keeping in mind when designing prompts for review analysis.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-meta">Hacker News · Nov 09 · 785 points, 488 comments</div>
