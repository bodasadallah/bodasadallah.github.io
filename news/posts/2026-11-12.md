<h2 class="dn-section">Models and releases</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Gemini Decisions API: Fast, calibrated multimodal decisions</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Google AI · Nov 11 · 478 points, 146 comments</div>
<p>Google released Gemini Decision 1, a 'system one' model that answers yes/no, multiple-choice and rubric-scoring questions with calibrated probabilities. It takes text, images, audio, video and PDFs, costs $0.10 per million input tokens and has about 200ms latency. Simon Willison notes it gives graded probabilities where Jev and OpenAI's model return 0 or 1. Calibrated rubric scoring at this price could be used directly as a baseline for review-score prediction.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xtour7/ai_slop_is_a_social_judgement_r/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AI ‘slop’ is a social judgement [R]</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">r/MachineLearning · Nov 11</div>
<p>A Nature Human Behaviour paper argues that AI 'slop' is a social judgement, made when a community decides some AI-generated content is unwanted, rather than something you can detect in the content itself. It is a useful framing for work on AI-written reviews and papers.</p>
