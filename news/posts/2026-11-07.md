<h2 class="dn-section">Big news</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xps7xu/iclr_2027s_new_14_scale_a_2_2_3_is_better_than_it/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ICLR 2027's new 1–4 scale: a 2, 2, 3 is better than it feels [P]</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">r/MachineLearning · Nov 07</div>
<p>ICLR 2027 now scores on a 1–4 scale (1 clear reject, 2 weak reject, 3 weak accept, 4 clear accept). A count of 73,410 public OpenReview reviews on 23,759 submissions finds 57% of reviews are a 2, only 1.5% are a 4, and the median paper averages exactly 2.0. The count comes from the team behind the scime.ai odds page, covers the first ~25k submissions in listing order rather than a random sample, and other threads report short, generic reviews and say there was no bidding this year. This is fresh score-distribution data, and a coarser scale, for your score-prediction and calibration work.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Models and releases</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://latent.space/p/ainews-inkling-2-small-17a-594b-trained-in-68-hours-on-12t-tokens-beating-muse-spark/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">[AINews] Inkling 2 Small: 17A-594B trained in 68 hours on 12T tokens, beating Muse Spark</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Latent Space · Nov 06</div>
<p>Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Small, an open-weight model with 17B active and 594B total parameters. It was pretrained from scratch in 68 hours on under 12T tokens in native NVFP4, with a single joint RL run, and is billed for its customizability. Latent Space reports that people call it the strongest Western open-weight model; it is competitive with, and often beats, Chinese models of the same size.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xpye7g/ant_ling_introduces_ling32flash_560b25b_native/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ant Ling introduces Ling-3.2-flash: 560B/25B native multimodal MoE with 1M context [N]</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">r/MachineLearning · Nov 07</div>
<p>Ant Ling released Ling-3.2-flash, a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 560B total and 25B active parameters and a 1M-token context window. On its own release evaluations it reports 92.21 on OmniDocBench 1.5 and 85.15 on CharXiv reasoning questions. Those document and scientific-chart scores matter if you parse papers as PDFs.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Research and techniques</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://bsky.app/profile/emollick.bsky.social/post/3mzomjgktvd2h" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Some new data from Anthropic looking at the impact of AI on the sciences by field: "With current capabilities, if adopted widely, AI could…</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Ethan Mollick on Bluesky · Nov 07 · 56 likes, 8 reposts</div>
<p>New Anthropic research, shared by Ethan Mollick, estimates that at current capabilities and with wide adoption, AI could take on 27–52% of the researcher time needed to produce a new study, with a median of 38% across fields. It is a company estimate broken down by scientific field, and useful context for work on how LLMs are changing scholarly writing and reviewing.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://hightouch.com/blog/llm-judges-identity-resolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener">A Jev that can ask Opus for help gives Opus accuracy at 4x lower cost</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hacker News · Nov 06 · 498 points, 118 comments</div>
<p>In a write-up from Hightouch, a cheaper model called Jev, used as an LLM judge for identity resolution, matched frontier-model accuracy at over 4x lower cost when it could hand hard cases to Claude Opus. GPT-6 Luna was also tested as a judge. It is a company blog post with 498 points on Hacker News, and a practical cascade design if you use LLM judges at scale.</p>
