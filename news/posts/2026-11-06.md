<div class="dn-meta">Latent Space · Nov 04</div>
<p>Several groups report that frontier LLMs can generate or edit the weights of small neural networks directly, with no gradient training and no tools. Latent Space says some claims were overstated and are disputed, but that the core result has been independently replicated more than once.</p>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xofjrn/dialfork_a_benchmark_for_procedural_hallucination/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DialFork: a benchmark for procedural hallucination in conversational agents [R]</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">r/MachineLearning · Nov 05</div>
<p>DialFork, from the Idiap Research Institute and Logitech, is a benchmark for 'procedural hallucination': an agent that is fluent and factual but stops following the procedure it was given. It forks simulated dialogues across 24 tasks in 12 domains into more than 36k test cases. It is a new axis for evaluating LLMs, beyond factual accuracy and correct tool use.</p>
</div>

<h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07489" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ReviewMap: Mapping Evaluative Acts and Automation Boundaries in Peer-Review Comments</a></h3>
<div class="dn-why">Classifies ICLR review comments into assessment vs calibration acts, with interpretable features usable for analysing review scores and rebuttals.</div>
<figure><img src="https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07489v1/figures/Fig1_ecosystem_original.png" alt="Main figure of ReviewMap: Mapping Evaluative Acts and Automation Boundaries in Peer-Review Comments" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Figure 1: The ReviewMap ecosystem: reviewers check assessments and ground value judgments; authors choose responses; area chairs inspect panel judgments and disagreements. These proposed affordances require users to inspect source comments and confirm attribute-based suggestions (§ 6 ).</figcaption></figure>
<details><summary>Abstract</summary><p>Peer-review comments check what a paper does and judge why it matters, but no existing scheme distinguishes these two evaluative acts. We argue that making these acts explicit and measuring how reliably LLMs identify them is a prerequisite for responsible LLM-assisted review analysis: humans need to inspect review judgments and their grounds. This defines a more honest mission for NLP in peer review than full automation. We present ReviewMap, a framework that classifies each comment as assessment of claims and scholarly standards or calibration of contribution value and venue fit, with finer-grained attributes applied conditionally by act type. Applied to 500 ICLR~2026 papers through multi-model LLM consensus, the act layer proves reliably automatable under the operational guideline (inter-model $\kappa \geq 0.75$), supported by five-human validation on 470 development comments, while the attribute layer requires human oversight, defining a measured automation boundary. At the corpus level, 17% of reviewer-paper instances contain no panel-coded calibration, and the panel-coded calibration share is nearly $3\times$ as high in Strengths as in Weaknesses. This boundary informs three affordances: a review guideline that operationalizes the assessment-then-calibration workflow for reviewers, a per-comment rebuttal strategy scaffold for authors, and a discussion-phase dashboard for area chairs.</p></details>
