<div class="dn-lede">This digest covers about a day, plus a few older items still drawing attention. For peer-review research, the big story is ICML 2027's new call for papers. It adds a two-stage process in which area chairs decide which papers go to full review, caps each author at 20 submissions, and requires every paper to name a reciprocal reviewer. Across the field, the main story is Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO after 33 years, along with Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen release.</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Big news</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xvkxbj/icml_2027_changes_d/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ICML 2027 Changes [D]</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">r/MachineLearning · Nov 13</div>
<p>According to a Reddit summary of the ICML 2027 call for papers, area chairs will decide in a first stage whether a paper goes to full review, and a senior area chair must confirm any rejection at that stage. Authors get a short justification by Feb 23. Each author may be on at most 20 submissions, each paper must name a reciprocal reviewer from its authors (at most 6 papers per reviewer), anyone with 3 or more submissions must serve on the program committee, and each author gets only one paper without an eligible reciprocal reviewer. This is a concrete venue experiment on reviewer load and triage that your peer-review work will probably want to track or cite.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://latent.space/p/ainews-jensen-huang-resigns-after-33-years-leading-nvidia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">[AINews] Jensen Huang resigns after 33 years leading NVIDIA</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Latent Space · Nov 13</div>
<p>Jensen Huang has stepped down as Nvidia's CEO, president and board member after 33 years, following reports of affairs, one of them with an alleged Chinese spy. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. A separate WSJ report, which got 669 points on Hacker News, alleges that Nvidia made the GPU shortage worse by funding startups that then bought its GPUs, and over 1,600 employees have commented on an internal post backing his return.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Models and releases</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/12/llm-gemini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">llm-gemini 0.37</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Simon Willison · Nov 12</div>
<p>Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, the cheapest model in the Gemini 4 family, at $1.50/$7.50 per million input/output tokens. It is now in Search and in Antigravity, where a new Orchestrator option pairs Hydrogen with other models. Simon Willison points out that it is only 25% cheaper than Carbon and Argon until those two double in price on December 1.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://latent.space/p/ainews-d-day-decision-models-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">[AINews] D-Day (Decision Models Day)</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Latent Space · Nov 12</div>
<p>Google announced Gemini Decision 1, which it describes as a calibrated multimodal decision model that can take up to 1,000 questions at once for $0.10 per million input tokens with about 200 ms latency. The benchmarks are Google's own. If the calibration claim holds, it could matter for score prediction and LLM-as-a-judge setups.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-meta">Hugging Face blog · Nov 13</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Research and techniques</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/xybench" target="_blank" rel="noopener">XYBench: Helping with the problem behind the question</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hugging Face blog · Nov 13</div>
<p>Ai2's XYBench tests whether LLMs notice the XY problem: a question that asks for a fix to the wrong thing instead of the real underlying problem. It won the Best Resource Award at EMNLP 2026. It is a well-built example of an evaluation resource that rewards answering the real need rather than the literal question.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-meta">Nathan Lambert on Bluesky · Nov 13 · 22 likes, 3 reposts</div>
<p>Nathan Lambert shared a new SAIF paper on the policy implications of model specs, the published documents that state how a model is supposed to behave, and how organisations can start writing one.
