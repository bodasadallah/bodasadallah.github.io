<div class="dn-lede">This digest covers about 29 hours. Two independent claimed proofs of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis appeared within hours of each other. Levent Alpöge of Anthropic released a Claude-driven proof as roughly 1.3GB of Lean code, and Alessandro Masullo released a 940-page, all-human draft that Brian Conrad has been reviewing for months. Neither is verified yet. The same window brought Anthropic's $2 trillion Nasdaq listing and Thinking Machines' open-weights Inkling 2 Mini.</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Big news</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://latent.space/p/ainews-riemann-hypothesis-proved-twice-independently/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">[AINews] Riemann Hypothesis proved...twice independently</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Latent Space · Nov 21</div>
<p>Two mathematicians separately claim proofs of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and neither claim is verified. Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) worked with Claude and released a note plus about 1.3GB of Lean code with no full paper; Elliot Glazer says the formal statement is GRH. Alessandro Masullo's 940-page, all-human draft had been under quiet review, and Ravi Vakil rushed out an announcement of it. Both stories topped Hacker News, and Timnit Gebru and others are contrasting the community's slow vetting with announce-first AI claims. For your work, the question is what it means to check a result no human will ever read in full.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropics-historic-debut-put-ais-promise-peril-wall-st-test-2026-11-19/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Anthropic’s Nasdaq debut</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hacker News · Nov 19 · 1134 points, 2302 comments</div>
<p>Anthropic went public on Nasdaq at about $2 trillion and raised $120 billion in the listing, according to Hard Fork's corrected figure. The Hacker News thread drew over 1,100 points and 2,300 comments. This is the largest business event in AI so far this year.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Inkling 2 Mini</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hacker News · Nov 20 · 1117 points, 211 comments</div>
<p>Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weights model small enough to run on a laptop. It loops its middle layers so it can reason more per token and reach answers in fewer tokens. The release passed 1,100 points on Hacker News, the Hugging Face repo has about 1,300 likes, and Unsloth's GGUF builds already have over 238k downloads.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Models and releases</h2>

<h3><a href="https://huggingface.co/blog/harness-adapters-at-scale" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Introducing harness adapters: adapt your favorite model to your harness to improve performance and cost</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hugging Face blog · Nov 20</div>
<p>Hugging Face introduced harness adapters: LoRA adapters trained with GRPO that tune an open model such as Qwen3.8-27B to a specific agent harness (opencode, pi), with the goal of improving performance and cost. Worth knowing if you run open models inside agent pipelines for review or citation analysis.</p>
