<h2 class="dn-section">Research and techniques</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://jack-clark.net/2026/11/09/import-ai-480-self-sustaining-space-economies-ai-for-science-progress-and-meta-builds-perfect-hands-for-a-superintelligence/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Import AI 480: Self-sustaining space economies; AI for science progress, and Meta builds perfect hands for a superintelligence</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Import AI · Nov 09</div>
<p>The startup Physical Superintelligence says its research system, running a non-public Anthropic model, proved the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture. The conjecture, open since 2008, asks when quantum computers can be exponentially faster than ordinary ones; the proof was complete on day three of the run and was checked in Lean. This is the company's own claim as reported in Import AI, and it is a useful case of AI-generated scientific claims that come with formal verification.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AI unlocks Gödel's notebooks, said to contain hundreds of proved theorems</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hacker News · Nov 13 · 430 points, 110 comments</div>
<p>The Guardian reports that AI was used to read Kurt Gödel's 1,009 pages of notebooks, much of them in an obsolete shorthand, which are said to contain hundreds of proved theorems. It is an example of AI reading historical scholarly documents, though the 'hundreds of theorems' is a claim the article reports, not one shown to be verified.</p>
</div>
