<div class="dn-lede">This covers about a day. The big story is "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming". Its authors claim GPT-6 Astra can read and directly edit the raw weights of a small model. They say it changed only 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B's weights and improved its math by up to 6x without any training. The claim comes from a blog post, has not been verified, and reached 2,400+ points on Hacker News. For your citation work, the item to read is a mass replication from the Zou group: it reports that recent LLMs no longer invent citations.</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Big news</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Hacker News · Nov 02 · 2444 points, 767 comments</div>
<p>A blog post co-authored by Alex Zhang claims GPT-6 Astra edited 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B's weights directly, with no gradients or training, and improved math by up to 6x and instruction following by 3x. It also claims the model can tell what a network was trained to do from its raw weights, and can write small game-playing networks that beat PPO-trained ones. None of this is verified yet; Ethan Mollick's reaction was "if this holds up, it is big." The post reached 2,444 points on Hacker News.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Models and releases</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://openai.com/index/lifeval" target="_blank" rel="noopener">LifeVal</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">OpenAI · Nov 02</div>
<p>OpenAI released LifeVal, a benchmark of how well AI helps people with everyday tasks such as finding childcare, managing finances and handling government paperwork. Mark Riedl called it "Goodhart's law as a service", which is relevant if you think about how benchmarks are designed and how they get gamed.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/2/llm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">llm 0.37</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Simon Willison · Nov 02</div>
<p>Simon Willison released llm 0.37, which adds the gpt-6.1 models, an easier way to configure OpenAI-compatible endpoints, and embeddings that handle mixed content types. He also reports that AI-assisted pull requests from 12 authors were easy to review once he used models to triage them, a small data point on AI-assisted review.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="dn-section">Research and techniques</h2>

<div class="dn-item">
<h3><a href="https://bsky.app/profile/emollick.bsky.social/post/3mzesndbbxs2l" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Here is a mass attempted replication of old findings of LLMs with new models. Much conventional wisdom is outdated. Recent models no longer…</a></h3>
<div class="dn-meta">Ethan Mollick on Bluesky · Nov 03 · 96 likes, 17 reposts</div>
<p>The Zou group re-ran older findings about LLMs on newer models. They report that recent models no longer invent citations and hallucinate and stereotype less, but are still sycophantic and lean left. The finding about fabricated references bears directly on your work on hallucinated citations, and it is worth checking how they measured it.</p>
</div>

<div class="dn-meta">Andrej Karpathy blog · Oct 31</div>
<p>Andrej Karpathy had Opus 5.5 build HackerTube, a YouTube-style front end for videos recommended on Hacker News.
