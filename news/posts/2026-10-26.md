<div class="dn-rank">#1 · Review Score Prediction · 8/10</div>
<h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31791" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PaperScorer: How Predictable Is Paper Acceptance?</a></h3>
<div class="dn-why">Predicts ICLR accept/reject from paper text or images; useful baseline for acceptance prediction, and models fail on mid-scored borderline papers.</div>
<figure><img src="https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31791v1/teaser_flow.png" alt="Main figure of PaperScorer: How Predictable Is Paper Acceptance?" loading="lazy"><figcaption>(a) PaperScorer predicts a paper’s decision from its text or visual view.</figcaption></figure>
<details><summary>Abstract</summary><p>Automated research is largely evaluated by benchmark performance, but the value of research rarely reduces to a single score. Humans judge a work's holistic contribution through peer review, which is hard to model because it is noisy and depends on many factors. However, what if, given only the paper, we could predict its peer-review decision directly? We introduce PaperScorer, a family of models that predict a paper's accept or reject decision from its extracted text or page screenshots, training only on the decision after removing author identity and formatting shortcuts. Vision models are the most predictive (68% accuracy on ICLR papers and 77% on ML papers from arXiv), beating text-only and frontier reasoning models. Unlike plain text, page images retain figures, layout, and presentation. This signal is robust, surviving smaller models and even compressing the whole paper into a single image. But accurate predictions are not evenly spread. PaperScorer is strongest on the lowest and highest rated papers, while papers rated in the middle stay unpredictable for our models and frontier systems alike. Additionally, its confidence is calibrated, so its most confident predictions are also its most accurate. Overall, PaperScorer shows how much of the decision a paper carries on its own, marking a first step toward AI-driven research evaluation.</p></details>

<div class="dn-paper">
<div class="dn-rank">#2 · Peer Review · 7/10</div>
<h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32114" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Tampered ML: Evaluating LLM Judgment of Machine Learning Research</a></h3>
<div class="dn-why">Counterfactual methodological-flaw benchmark for LLM judges of ML research, close to automatic-reviewer flaw-detection evaluations.</div>
<details><summary>Abstract</summary><p>One approach to accelerating progress on AI alignment is to automate parts of alignment research itself. Doing so requires systems that can perform reliably on hard-to-supervise research tasks, including identifying flaws in research proposals and evaluating whether a proposed experiment supports its intended claim. To evaluate this capability, we introduce Tampered ML, a benchmark of 1,858 item-pairs, each consisting of an experiment proposal reconstructed from a published machine learning paper and a minimally edited counterfactual that introduces a targeted methodological flaw. Models must identify the more methodologically sound proposal in both presentation orders. A human validation study on a stratified sample estimates that 86% of items are valid (95% CI: 81-91%). Frontier models perform above chance but remain far from reliable (with Opus 5 achieving 77.9% accuracy). They are highly sensitive to the order of presentation and favor flawed designs that appear more specific, transparent, or conventional.</p></details>

<div class="dn-paper">
<div class="dn-rank">#3 · NLP · 5/10</div>
<h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31810" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PaperFormalizer: Author-in-the-Loop Autoformalization of Research Papers in Lean</a></h3>
<div class="dn-why">Agentic auditing and Lean formalization of research-paper claims; related to paper consistency verification.</div>
<figure><img src="https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31810v1/ui_page.png" alt="Main figure of PaperFormalizer: Author-in-the-Loop Autoformalization of Research Papers in Lean" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Figure 2: The page of the case-study paper (Section 5 ) after its run; the viewer carries the name of the public website, PaperFormalizer. The left column tracks the run’s stages (V1 is Stage 1, V1.5 Stage 2, F1–F1.5 Stage 3, F2 Stage 4, F2.5–F4 Stage 5, and F5–V6 Stage 6), gives the paper’s content</figcaption></figure>
<details><summary>Abstract</summary><p>Even a carefully proofread paper can leave small gaps in its proofs or its stated assumptions. We introduce PaperFormalizer, a Lean-based agentic pipeline that helps authors find and close such gaps in their own papers and turn each paper into a Lean-verified version. Starting from the LaTeX source, PaperFormalizer transcribes the paper into a dependency graph of statements, audits each statement in natural language for faithfulness, ambiguity and correctness, and then formalizes and proves the statements in Lean against Mathlib, optional domain libraries and the author's personal toolkit. Only the author knows what a statement is meant to say and how a formalization gap in a result is best fixed. The pipeline therefore stops and asks the author whenever a statement admits more than one reading, presents every repair, in natural language and TeX symbols, as a proposal that the author can accept, decline or redirect, and leaves to the author every decision that could potentially change what the paper claims. The output is an annotated and revised version of the paper, a report of the changes, and Lean code in which each declaration is linked to the statement it formalizes. The author's toolkit grows as more papers are formalized, and authors can share their verified papers so that researchers who build on them can reuse their Lean code. By making verified papers practical to produce, PaperFormalizer aims to make research that rests on mathematical proofs more robust. PaperFormalizer runs inside a coding agent (Claude Code or Codex) and is available at https://github.com/Jiyuan-Tan/PaperFormalizer. Papers with Lean code shared by users are available at https://paperformalizer.org.</p></details>
